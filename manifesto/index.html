Engineers in charge

A manifesto for engineers working with AI agents.

This is for engineers who want what AI makes possible without handing over the wheel. It’s what I’ve come to believe after months of getting it wrong, and it’s what I’m building toward.

What changed

AI has caused a temporal shift. Work that used to take a day or two now takes under an hour. The work didn’t get easier; the slow parts did. Reading docs for a system you’ve never touched, hunting for the right setting, writing the boilerplate.

Context switching should be nearly free. You already know how these systems work. What wears you down is relearning every detail just to finish one task. An agent can carry that detail for you. You still need to understand how the system works. You don’t need to memorize every vendor’s knobs.

Less grind, more of the work that matters. Hand the tedious parts to the agent and spend your time on design, judgment and the problems only you can solve.

What doesn’t change

Models are great at assumptions. Assumptions aren’t decisions. An agent will happily fill every gap with a confident guess. Context, steering, correction and refinement are the job now, and that’s why engineers aren’t being replaced.

You own what ships, no matter who typed it. If an agent wrote the change, it still goes out under your name.

Every agent decision is on the record. Even the small break-fix calls an agent makes on its own get logged and can be reviewed. That doesn’t mean a person approves every step. Review scales with risk: small fixes are recorded, risky changes wait for a human.

An agent is just another user of your platform. It gets the same well-paved paths and the same firm boundaries as everyone else. Give engineers room to move fast, and put the limits where they protect everyone.

How to do it well

Fit AI to your workflow, not your workflow to AI. Same tools, same reviews, same on-call. The pace of the work changes, not its shape. It’s a smaller shift than most teams expect.

Your tools should bend to you. The best engineers I know are all opinionated about their tools, and they’re right to be. Agent tools should be customizable to how you work, stable enough to trust, and never tied to a single model.

Doing it safely is the hard part. Speed is easy to demo. Guardrails, audit trails and sensible defaults are what make it last. That’s the part I care most about, and it’s the part I’m building.

Let the agent drive the work. Keep your hands on the wheel.
